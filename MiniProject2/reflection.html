01 · What I built

A research pipeline and dashboard for public legal-AI discourse

I built DocketSignal, a small research pipeline and static dashboard for looking at public Reddit discourse about legal AI. The tool collects or loads posts and comments, cleans the text, assigns early qualitative themes, adds sentiment and rhetorical-frame tags, and turns the results into CSV files, excerpts, memos, charts, and a public-facing dashboard.

The audience I had in mind was a legal-tech researcher, UX researcher, or product collaborator who wants to understand what people are publicly worried about. I also built design thinking, competency, and reflection pages because the project needed to show more than the dashboard. It needed to show why I made the choices I made.

02 · Decisions

Reddit-only data, clear boundaries, and visible evidence

I chose a Cursor + Python project because the hard part of this project was the data and analysis pipeline. A full web app would have been more work than the project needed. A static dashboard was enough to make the work visible, especially for the MP2 gallery.

I kept Reddit as the live data source because it has public discussion from lawyers, law students, legal-tech users, and adjacent professionals. I dropped broader web scraping and LinkedIn-style collection because those sources would have raised more provenance and terms-of-service issues. I also made a design decision to keep evidence close to each theme so the viewer can check what the summary is based on.

03 · What I would do differently

Add validation loops and preserve collection runs

I would add a human validation loop inside the tool. Right now the theme labels are transparent, and the excerpts are inspectable, but the actual validation still happens outside the interface. A stronger version would let the researcher accept, reject, rename, or merge codes in the dashboard.

I would also preserve collection runs instead of overwriting the same output files. If I ran the Reddit collector every week, I would want each run to keep its own timestamp, query terms, subreddit list, and row counts. The design system is another place I would test more, especially with legal researchers who may want a denser review mode.

04 · Competency demonstration

Computational fluency, data ethics, qualitative judgment, and design thinking

This project demonstrates computational fluency, data ethics, qualitative research judgment, communication design, and design thinking across disciplines. The computational part is visible in src/pipeline.py, src/collect_reddit.py, and src/qualitative.py. Those files collect, clean, code, summarize, and export the corpus.

The cross-discipline piece is important to me. From law, I brought attention to evidence, risk, confidentiality, citations, billing, and governance. From UX research, I brought coding, synthesis, stakeholder framing, and interface decisions. DocketSignal shows how those habits can work together in a product research artifact.
